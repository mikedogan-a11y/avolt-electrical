## 1 — Loan file pipeline, intake to settlement  *(Phase 1 — send in full + attach the gate)*

Hi Peter,

Here's how a file actually moves, intake to settlement.

**Typical deal journey.** A file usually starts with a referral (accountant, agent, existing
client) or a direct/website enquiry. I have the first conversation, work out the scenario,
objective, lending purpose and whether it's worth progressing. We then collect documents and
complete the fact-find/interview. Details go into our **WordPress proposal builder**, which
generates the **Proposal Disclosure** and the **Client R&O** document. I review the file against my
pre-lodgement gate before lender selection and lodgement (we lodge through **Mercury/ApplyOnline**
and the lender portal).

Indicative timeframes (happy for these to be firmed up with you in Discovery):
- Enquiry → qualification: same day to a few days, depending on client responsiveness.
- Document collection / fact-find: a few days; longer where self-employed income docs are
  incomplete.
- Assessment, servicing and lender selection: ~1–3 days once docs are in.
- Application prep and lodgement: ~1–2 days once structure is clear.
- Lodgement → conditional → unconditional → settlement: lender/valuation/conveyancer dependent.

**Most time-consuming / repetitive.** Chasing missing documents, checking whether documents are
current, re-keying the same client details across systems, writing file notes, checking compliance
requirements, tracking lender conditions, and keeping clients updated.

**Where files stall.** Document collection, self-employed income verification, servicing questions,
valuations, lender conditions, conveyancer delays, and clients being slow to sign/return items.

**Where documents arrive.** Mostly email, sometimes text/WhatsApp, and some saved straight to
Google Drive. Saloni and the broker-support team chase and keep files moving; I step in where the
relationship or the issue needs me.

**The proposal builder.** Built and maintained by Prajwol on our Elementor site. It captures the
client scenario, objectives, loan purpose, income, liabilities, security, the lender
recommendation, BID rationale, risks and challenges, and generates the Proposal Disclosure and
Client R&O. Some data is still typed in from client documents, emails and Mercury — though we are
**already automating that step** with a proposal bot that fills the form and builds the PDF.

**Re-keying.** The same fields get re-entered between Mercury, the proposal builder, lender
portals/ApplyOnline, servicing calculators, QED and Drive file notes — borrower names, contacts,
employment/income, liabilities, property, loan amount, purpose and product.

**Servicing & lender selection.** I use a **QED servicing spreadsheet** for transparency, lender
policy tools and calculators, **Quickli** where useful, and direct BDM/policy knowledge. The manual
part is interpreting policy, entering income/liabilities, checking add-backs, confirming niches and
making sure the recommendation aligns with the client's objectives and my BID obligations. The
**lender choice and rationale are mine** and recorded in the assessment notes and Proposal
Disclosure; support staff prepare the file but the credit recommendation stays with me.

**Post-lodgement tracking.** Saloni and the team track conditions, valuations and approvals through
Mercury, lender portals and our file notes; I'm involved for decisions, escalations and client
conversations.

**First thing to take off the team's plate.** Document chasing and file-status tracking, then
consistency-checking between the documents, notes and the application. What must stay human: credit
judgement, lender recommendation, BID rationale, client strategy, exceptions, sensitive
conversations, and anything that could amount to credit advice.

**For your Phase 1 build, I'll send separately:** my **15-point pre-lodgement gate**, my
**assessment-note standard**, my **Priority Pyramid (policy → features → cost)**, the
**vulnerability assessment** and the **BID conclusion** structure — these are the standard the audit
agent should enforce.
